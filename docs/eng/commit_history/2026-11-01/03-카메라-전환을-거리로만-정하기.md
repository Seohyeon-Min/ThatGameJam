# Camera change set by distance only, to cut the stutter and the lag

## Why

User feedback. Framing the bird and the girl, then the bird flying off and the camera turning to the bird alone, the camera stutters a little and follows late.

The previous commit's (02) test log showed why. As the bird flew off at 5 m/s, the camera point's speed jumped 0 → 1.6 m/s (leaving the dead zone) and 2.4 → 4.8 m/s (reaching the screen-edge limit). Then the 2 s ease went on, so the camera slid on for 0.9 s after the bird stopped. The 10 m exit distance came later than the screen-edge limit, so the limit was always hit first.

## What changed

| What | Where |
|------|-------|
| Removed the two states ("both" / "bird alone") and the 2 s timed ease. The weight comes from the bird–girl distance only: 1 within `cameraTogetherNearDistance` (4 m), 0 past `cameraTogetherFarDistance` (14 m), SmoothStep between. When the bird stops, the target stops | `BirdCamera.cs` `Framed` |
| Past the dead zone (1.5 m), the part beyond is closed exponentially over `cameraTogetherFollowTime` (0.2 s). Before, all of it was followed in that frame, so it set off with a jolt. The zone and the time scale with the weight, so the bird alone is exactly in the middle | `BirdCamera.cs` `Framed` |
| The screen-edge limit stays, without the weight factor. It is now only a safety net | `BirdCamera.cs` `Framed` |
| Removed `cameraTogetherEnterDistance`, `cameraTogetherExitDistance`, `cameraTogetherEaseTime`. Added `cameraTogetherNearDistance`, `cameraTogetherFarDistance`, `cameraTogetherFollowTime` | `GameTuning.cs` |

## Important decisions

- 4 m and 14 m came from a Python simulation. At 6 m / 12 m the screen-edge limit was hit for 15 frames; at 4 m / 14 m, never. In exchange, while the bird flies off the camera point moves faster than the bird (about twice), since it must come over from the girl's side to the bird's. Its speed changes continuously.

## Left
