# Made the jump animation carry the actual climb, not play after it (follow-up to [07](07-통나무-오르내릴때-점프-애니메이션.md))

## Why

07 added Jump, but the repo owner pointed out: "let's actually have the jump animation do the climbing up/down the log — right now it's just jumping on top of it." Correct call. Re-checking the real coordinates showed the `BalanceBeamStart`/`End` markers sit on the point **already at that height** (`Point_0` is ground level (y0) yet marked Start; `Point_1` is already on the log (y0.6)) — the actual height change happened in the *following* segment (`Point_0`→`Point_1`, `Point_2`→`Point_3`) at ordinary walk speed, sliding up/down. Jump just replayed in place after the climb had already happened.

## What changed

| What | Where |
|------|-------|
| Replaced playing `PlayJump()` (in place) right at the marker point with a `jumpNextSegment` flag, so the **next** segment itself is handled as a jump | `Baby.cs` (`WakeAndWalk`) |
| Replaced `PlayJump()` with `JumpTo(target)`: instead of standing still, it actually moves from the current position to the next point via `Vector3.Lerp` (height included) over the Jump clip's length (1.5s), then crossfades to `Walk`/`BalanceWalk` once it lands | `Baby.cs` (new `JumpTo`, replaces the old `PlayJump`) |

## Key decisions

- Didn't guess from reading the code that the marker point and the actual height-changing segment were offset by one — printed every route point's position to confirm (`Point_0` y0·Start / `Point_1` y0.6 / `Point_2` y0.6·End / `Point_3` y0 — the markers sit on the point right *before* climbing up or right *before* climbing down, not on the point where the height actually changes).
- So the right design was "jump the segment *after* the marker," not "jump at the marker." Solved it by delaying `jumpNextSegment` by one loop tick.
- Folded the LeaningLog case into the same pattern: the moment the blocker opens is still at the waiting point (ground level); the segment right after that (waiting point → first point on the log) is the real climb, so it goes through `jumpNextSegment` too.
- Inside `JumpTo`, the `Walk`/`BalanceWalk` crossfade happens *after* the jump lands (so the walk-cycle animation starts right as the feet touch down); only the `Jump` state crossfades in at the start.

## Remaining

- Play Mode dropped out on its own once during testing for an unknown, non-reproducible reason unrelated to compile errors — worth flagging if it happens again.
- The jump's actual path is a straight `Vector3.Lerp`. If the Jump clip itself has a curved crouch-leap-land arc, the body's visual motion may not quite match a perfectly linear root position — worth an eyes-on check, and an easing curve if it reads as mismatched.
