# The camera follows the bird and stops in front of devices (ADR 0005)

## Why

After looking at the grey-box stages, the user said the camera should follow the bird by default. Where there is a device, the camera should not move further forward even if the bird does, and the limit should go away when the device is solved. Whether to keep the baby on screen in front and behind, and when to lift the limit, were proposed by the AI and accepted by the user.

## Key decisions

- At first the bird's click target was clamped to the area at that moment. The area moves with the bird, so a far click stopped the bird at the edge of the first area and the player had to click again. Now the target is kept and only the bird's position is clamped every frame
- Limit lines are judged by Z only, assuming stages run along +Z. All current stages do

## Cross-model review

Codex. Correctness (update order, stage transitions, perching and being pushed while the area moves, conflicting clamps), robustness, and security lenses. Scope: the `.cs` files in the staged diff.

| Finding | Resolution |
|---------|------------|
| blocker. The old scenes' `brain` and `CameraZone` references are not migrated to the new field, so starting a stage throws | Dismissed. The review saw only `.cs`. Both test scenes were rebuilt with the builder and are in this commit. They ran fine in Play Mode |
| warn. The real camera lags 0.4 s, so the bird can leave the real screen | Dismissed. In front of a limit line the followed point stops, so there is no lag. While the point moves, the bird is near the middle of the screen. In testing the bird stayed on screen |
| warn. The three clamps are applied in order, so a later one can break an earlier one | Dismissed. The order is intended: the limit line wins over the baby range, which wins over the stage area. The baby is always behind a closed limit line, and stage areas are laid out to contain the baby range |
